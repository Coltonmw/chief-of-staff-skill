# Proposed weekly reports

For a principal running several client engagements with a small team of people and agents. Every report leads with decisions needed, uses facts only, and ends with one question about what to change next week.

**Start with these three (★):** Monday plan, Decisions needed, Project status roll-up. Add the others one at a time once those feel right.

---

## ★ 1. Monday plan
- **Purpose:** Set the week. Top priorities, decisions waiting, what's on the calendar.
- **Sources:** Project list, calendar, last week's reports, open action items.
- **Cadence:** Monday, start of the working day.
- **Format:** Decisions needed, then the top 3 to 5 priorities with owners, then calendar highlights with prep notes.
- **Length cap:** One screen (about 15 lines).
- **What makes it useful:** The principal knows in two minutes what matters this week and what only they can do.

## ★ 2. Decisions needed
- **Purpose:** One list of every open decision or approval waiting on the principal.
- **Sources:** Meeting summaries, client threads, project list, negotiation prep sheets.
- **Cadence:** Friday (and folded into the Monday plan).
- **Format:** Numbered list: decision, options, recommendation, who's waiting, deadline.
- **Length cap:** 10 items. If more, the top 10 by deadline and a count of the rest.
- **What makes it useful:** Nothing stalls quietly. Each item can be answered in one line.

## ★ 3. Project status roll-up
- **Purpose:** Every active project in one table.
- **Sources:** Project list, RAID logs, team updates.
- **Cadence:** Friday.
- **Format:** Table: project, client, status (on track / at risk / blocked / done), next step, owner, due. At-risk and blocked first.
- **Length cap:** One table, plus up to 5 lines of notes.
- **What makes it useful:** Status changes stand out because the shape never changes.

## 4. Pipeline and proposals
- **Purpose:** Where every prospect, proposal, and bid stands.
- **Sources:** CRM or pipeline sheet, email threads, proposal docs.
- **Cadence:** Friday.
- **Format:** Table: prospect, stage, last contact, next step, owner, date. Proposals awaiting the principal's approval at the top.
- **Length cap:** One table.
- **What makes it useful:** No lead goes cold for lack of a follow-up. No proposal sits unsigned without anyone noticing.

## 5. Cash and invoices (facts only)
- **Purpose:** Money in and out, as recorded. No projections.
- **Sources:** Invoicing tool, bank or accounting records, timesheets.
- **Cadence:** Friday.
- **Format:** Invoices sent, paid, overdue (with age); bills due in the next 14 days; hours logged but not yet invoiced.
- **Length cap:** 10 lines.
- **What makes it useful:** Overdue invoices and unbilled hours surface every week, before they become a problem.

## 6. Risks and blockers
- **Purpose:** What could go wrong across all projects, and what is stuck now.
- **Sources:** RAID logs, project list, team check-ins.
- **Cadence:** Friday (merged into the roll-up until it needs its own page).
- **Format:** Blockers first (what, who can unblock, since when), then top risks with plan.
- **Length cap:** 8 items.
- **What makes it useful:** The principal sees problems while they are still cheap to fix.

## 7. Team throughput and review lag
- **Purpose:** How fast work moves through the team.
- **Sources:** Task tracker, pull requests or review queue, action items from meetings.
- **Cadence:** Friday.
- **Format:** Items finished, items opened, items waiting on review (and for how long), overdue actions by owner.
- **Length cap:** 10 lines.
- **What makes it useful:** Shows where work waits, so the fix is a process change, not a push.

## 8. Wins
- **Purpose:** What shipped, what clients praised, who did great work.
- **Sources:** Project list, client messages, meeting summaries.
- **Cadence:** Friday.
- **Format:** Bullets, each naming the person and the effect.
- **Length cap:** 5 bullets.
- **What makes it useful:** Material for recognition, case studies, and the principal's own morale.

## 9. Next week's calendar prep
- **Purpose:** Get ahead of next week's meetings.
- **Sources:** Calendar, project list, client threads.
- **Cadence:** Friday afternoon.
- **Format:** Per meeting: goal, who's there, what to bring, open actions with that party.
- **Length cap:** 3 lines per meeting.
- **What makes it useful:** No meeting starts cold.

## 10. Follow-ups owed
- **Purpose:** Everything the firm promised someone and hasn't delivered yet.
- **Sources:** Meeting summaries, client threads, concern logs.
- **Cadence:** Wednesday.
- **Format:** Promise, to whom, owner, due.
- **Length cap:** 10 items.
- **What makes it useful:** Keeps the firm's word reliable, which is most of its reputation.

## 11. Skill and report feedback log
- **Purpose:** What changed in the reports and this skill, and why.
- **Sources:** The principal's feedback, the changelog.
- **Cadence:** Monthly, or with the last report of the month.
- **Format:** Date, feedback, change made, version.
- **Length cap:** 10 lines.
- **What makes it useful:** The reports keep improving instead of drifting.
